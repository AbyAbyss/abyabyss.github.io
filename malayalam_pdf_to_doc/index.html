Why does Malayalam text from my PDF paste as junk?

Many Malayalam books, magazines, newspapers and church publications were typeset with legacy fonts such as ML-TT Karthika, Revathi, Ambili, Vinay, Thunchan or Indulekha. These fonts draw Malayalam letters on top of English character codes, so when you copy the text you get something like ssZh-Øns‚ instead of ദൈവത്തിന്റെ. This tool reads those codes and converts them to proper Unicode Malayalam that you can search, edit and paste anywhere: Word, Google Docs, WhatsApp or a website.

How to convert a Malayalam PDF to Unicode

  1. Drop your PDF into the box at the top of the page.
  2. The font encoding is detected automatically (ML-TT, Windows or Mac character sets).
  3. Check the side-by-side preview, then download a Word (DOCX), TXT or Markdown file, or copy the text.

Is my PDF uploaded?

No. The PDF is opened and converted inside your browser. Nothing is sent to a server, and once the page has loaded it works offline.

Does it work on scanned PDFs?

No. A scanned PDF is a picture of the page with no text inside, so it needs OCR (optical character recognition). This tool is for PDFs where you can select text, even if the copied text looks garbled. It is more accurate than OCR for those files because it decodes the exact characters the typesetter used.

Does it keep bold, headings and alignment?

Yes. With Keep formatting selected, the Word file keeps bold and italic text, headings, font sizes, centred and right-aligned lines, and paragraph indents from the PDF.
